I am a postdoctoral fellow in the [ACDS Lab](https://lab-idar.gatech.edu/) at Georgia Tech, supervised by **Evangelos Theodorou**. I received my Ph.D. from Princeton University, where I worked in the [IRoM Lab](https://irom-lab.princeton.edu/) with **Anirudha Majumdar**. Before that, I earned my B.S.E. in Electrical Engineering and M.S.E. in Robotics from the University of Pennsylvania, where my thesis was supervised by **Daniel E. Koditschek**.

My research develops principled methods that identify and exploit <span class="highlight">task-relevant</span> structure in the stochastic optimal control</span> and learning problems found in robotics. I use variational principles from fields like <span class="highlight">information theory</span> and <span class="highlight">statistical mechanics</span> to formally characterize what problem structure is relevant to a task---and then build that characterization into algorithms with <span class="highlight">provable guarantees</span>, both analytically and through data-driven methods. This perspective applies to both <span class="highlight">sensing</span> (i.e., what information should a controller use?) and <span class="highlight">computation</span> (i.e., how should an algorithm be structured for a specific problem class?) It also yields <span class="highlight">fundamental limits</span> on achievable performance: bounds on what any controller can accomplish given a system's dynamics and sensors.

Recently, I co-wrote a grant proposal on developing a generalization theory for diffusion models, which is actively being funded as part of the [DARPA AIQ Program](https://www.darpa.mil/research/programs/aiq-artificial-intelligence-quantified). I also teach <span class="highlight">AE4803 RO2: Robotics &amp; Autonomy</span>, an undergraduate robotics course, at Georgia Tech.
